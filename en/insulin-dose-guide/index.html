How to calculate an insulin dose: carb ratio, ISF & correction

⚠️ For information only. Your carb ratio, ISF and target glucose must be set by your diabetes care team. Do not change your treatment on your own.

With multiple daily injections or a pump, the mealtime (bolus) dose has two parts: insulin to cover the food and insulin to correct high glucose.

Insulin-to-carb ratio (ICR)

ICR tells how many grams of carbs one unit of rapid-acting insulin covers (e.g. 1 U : 10 g). Some countries use carb units instead — 1 CU (XE/BE) = 10–12 g — and express the ratio as units per CU. Ratios often differ between breakfast, lunch and dinner.

Meal dose = carbs (g) ÷ ICR   or   CU × units per CU

Insulin sensitivity factor (ISF)

ISF (correction factor) is how much 1 unit lowers your glucose — e.g. 50 mg/dL (2.8 mmol/L) per unit.

Correction = (current glucose − target) ÷ ISF

Worked example

mg/dLmmol/L
Carbs60 g, ICR 1 U : 12 g → 5 U

ISF45 per U2.5 per U
Correction(200 − 110) ÷ 45 = 2 U(11.1 − 6.1) ÷ 2.5 = 2 U

If your pen doses in 0.5 or 1 U steps, round the result. Rounding down is usually safer, especially for children. InsulinCalc shows both options with a glucose forecast for each.

Insulin on board

Rapid-acting insulin works for about 3–5 hours. Correcting again too soon without accounting for insulin on board (IOB) is a common cause of lows. InsulinCalc warns you about active insulin from your log.

How to check your ratios

  • Carb ratio: if you start in range, glucose should return to about target 2–4 hours after the meal.
  • ISF: check with a correction without food — how far glucose falls in 3–4 hours per unit given.

The Reports page estimates both automatically from your 2 h and 4 h readings: from clean events, per meal and with multiple regression — ready to discuss with your doctor.

About the “500” and “1800” rules

Rules of thumb (500 ÷ total daily dose for ICR, 1800 ÷ TDD for ISF in mg/dL, or 100 ÷ TDD in mmol/L) are starting points for clinicians. They are often inaccurate in children, teens and during the honeymoon phase; personal ratios come from your log.

Common mistakes

  • Under- or over-counting carbs (drinks, sauces, fruit).
  • Giving a bolus when low — below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) treat the low first.
  • Stacking corrections without considering insulin on board.
  • Random check times instead of 2 h and 4 h — this makes ratios impossible to assess.
